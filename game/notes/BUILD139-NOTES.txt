SKATING-AREA MILESTONE - FIRST PLAYABLE PASS
Physics now maintains an absolute airborne wheel-contact height. A ramp, dip or raised surface underneath no longer changes the trajectory in midair. Landing uses the actual rendered road, rolled curb, footpath/returns and plywood support; decorative solid tops are excluded from the skating support sampler.
Natural edge departures drop under gravity. Exposed high steps collide rather than teleporting the board onto their top. Ramp takeoff wins over the generic edge-drop rule at the lip. Surface slope gravity affects rolling momentum on the plywood and curb/path grades.
Airborne mesh support uses the absolute trajectory too, preventing a second terrain-height offset. Existing landing animation and scoring rules consume the corrected touchdown event.
Free-skate camera heading responds sooner, look-ahead scales with speed, and the camera clears terrain. Added original wheel wear around the plywood/run-out and fine seams at existing path mouths. Braking and slides leave session-local wheel scuffs, bounded to 1,024 segments and two extra scene draw calls total for live/static marks.
No ramp enlargement, route relocation, new free-ride entry, new action buttons or new trick permissions.
