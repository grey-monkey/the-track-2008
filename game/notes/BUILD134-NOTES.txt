THE TRACK 2008 — BUILD134
October 15, 2026. Local playtest, based on the owner's Build133 handoff.

DIRECTION
The supplied handoff is now the working code and art foundation. The rejected
fresh block-based prototype is not used. The personal Track setting, crew,
Mercedes, boards, music, sunset, road details and rope dynamics are retained.
Target: the movement readability and character of a mid-2000s skating game.
This is a movement iteration, not a claim of THPS4-equivalent animation quality.

IMPLEMENTED
Four stable fall families: low-speed trip, hip slide, backward body recoil,
and high-speed shoulder roll. Actual ground contact starts impact and friction;
airborne motion no longer slows as if already sliding. Rotation is finite.
The old universal sideways rotations and invented contact time are removed.
Body poses settle horizontally. Slow falls no longer give the board a full spin.
Original body-ground support and impact audio remain active.

Foot pushing uses the handoff character's leg weights and three articulated
segments. The support foot stays planted; the pushing shoe turns toward travel,
presses backwards, then returns. A 0.9-second cycle applies force only during
the ground stroke. Both stances and four playable riders are supported.
Corrected the compacted ankle-skin mapping discovered during visual review.

Free ride can continue from rest, with pushing and braking; the car keeps
circulating during free riding and wipeouts. Existing permitted ollies/kickflips
work while released. Landing compression varies with impact and airtime.
Release preserves momentum and keeps the existing unilateral rope spring.

The camera frames the rider and approaching pavement; vertical FOV adapts to
portrait/landscape. The original scene fills the window instead of being locked
inside a narrow portrait strip. Touch buttons support simultaneous steering and
actions. Pause/resume works, hidden tabs pause, orientation changes preserve play.

CONTROLS
A/D or left/right arrows: carve. W: push after release. S: brake after release.
Space: crouch; release Space for a permitted trick. E/up arrow: release rope.
Escape: pause/resume. Touch: drag to carve, or use the steering buttons;
hold Push/Brake/Crouch buttons. Existing flick-up release remains available.
After a wipeout, Another tow restarts from the crash's route position.
The original rider/board trick restrictions remain as specified by the handoff.

OBSERVED THPS4 EVIDENCE
Decoded bails.q lines16–61/219–229 choose runout/directional fall families.
Lines314–441 orient to velocity, wait for ground, apply brief heavy friction,
then slide using speed checks, followed by a distinct get-up clip.
Lines730–768 keep an airborne fall active until actual ground contact.
tricks.qb: push uses NoRestart and completion checks; landing setup begins
with roughly 0.2 seconds of airtime remaining and branches by airtime.
The numeric speed thresholds in those scripts have unverified physical units.
We did not copy them directly into the Track's meter-based simulation.

The 56 supplied bail containers have 50 bones and pass allocation/EOF structure
checks using the documented SKA layout. Observed clip lengths: Rolling2.7333s,
Shoulders3.4333s, Hips1.5333s, RunOut2.5s, forward/hip get-up1.5333s.
These are clip lengths, not necessarily total runtime sequence durations.

WHAT IS NOT YET IMPLEMENTED
These are newly authored Track motions informed by the scripts, not converted
THPS4 animation clips. The handoff has four baked crash poses, not a complete
runtime skeleton. Detailed running stumbles, hand planting, limb choreography,
board retrieval and articulated get-up clips still need animation work.
No NPC rope hand-off or complete THPS-style trick/combo system was added.
Physical phone/GPU performance has not been established by desktop emulation.
No public deployment has been made. Existing asset credits remain in the game.

NEXT ANIMATION STRATEGY
1. Keep this restored handoff as the baseline, including its original assets.
2. Decode a small representative THPS clip set into a private reference scene:
   forward trip, shoulder roll, backward slip, runout and get-up. Verify bones,
   coordinates, key timing and root displacement against visible playback.
3. Build corresponding articulated poses for Track characters, with hand/foot
   contacts and transitions; replace the four-pose fall approximation by family.
4. Review side-by-side motion at normal speed and slow speed, then validate the
   full tow -> release -> push -> carve -> fall -> recover loop on real phones.
5. Expand visual treatment and skating interactions on that validated base.

VALIDATION
108 fall-motion checks, 256 mesh-support checks, and final body-height checks
across all four characters and both stances. Five articulated-push tests cover
bone lengths, phase endpoints, ankle mapping and cancellation. Ride tests cover
release momentum, free ollie, no airborne push, pushing from rest, braking,
contact and finite crash completion, and an independently continuing car.
Browser review: desktop, portrait phone and landscape phone layouts; actual
fall sequence screenshots; Dog and Sally pushing; pause/resume; touch actions;
rotation preserves time, free-ride state and release. Final main-browser pass
reported no page errors or missing resources. This does not establish Safari
compatibility or performance on a physical mobile device.
